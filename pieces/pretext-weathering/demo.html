The ford

Trav­el­ler, this is the ford at Eller­beck. The river here runs wide and shal­low over gravel for forty paces, and in summer a child may cross it dry-shod on the stones. Do not trust it after rain. When the water stands above the third step below this stone, wait, for it rises faster than a horse can walk, and it has taken eleven souls that we know of. The well behind the wall is sweet and free to all who pass. Drink, water your beasts, and hang the bucket on its hook for the next who comes. The people of this parish cut this stone in the year 1726, and bound them­selves and those after them to keep the steps, the well and the bucket for as long as the river runs. If you can read this, we have kept our word.
