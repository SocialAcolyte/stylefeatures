# pushou: the pick

**Pick: Freer F1917.18, *Mask and ring handle*, China, Warring States period, 5th–4th century BCE.** Bronze with
gold and silver inlay, 7.7 × 5.4 × 2.2 cm. National Museum of Asian Art (Freer Gallery), gift of Charles Lang Freer,
CC0. Studio photograph FS-7729_16, 4200 × 3150; the object alone is about 1650 × 2380 px (about 310 px/cm).

## Why this one

- **The moving part is the beautiful part.** The ring carries the inlay: gold and silver cloud spirals hammered into
  its dark bronze. When the ring tilts and drops, the spirals are what moves.
- **The right period and the real craft.** Warring States, the brief's own period, and *cuo jin yin* (錯金銀): channels
  cast into the bronze, gold and silver hammered in and burnished flush. The mask's brows and nose ridge and the
  nose loop's band are inlaid in the same way.
- **It reads at 40 px.** A dark mask and a dark ring on the paper, with gold glints, are still a knocker at 40 px
  (see the scale test). The pale bronzes fade into the paper there.
- **Sharp and clean.** A studio shot on a white sweep (engine PASS: no enlargement, real grain). There are no
  reflections, and dark bronze on white mattes easily.
- **Frontal, with a clean hinge.** The nose bends down into a loop with a gold band, and the ring hangs through it.
  The loop lies *in front of* the ring's top, so the ring cuts out whole below it and the loop stays with the mask.
- **Measured.** The catalogue gives a depth of 2.2 cm, so the loop holds the ring about 1.3 cm off the door. That
  sets the ring's resting lean (about 12°, its foot touching the door) and the hinge line. No side view of this
  object exists, and the depth stands in for one.

## What it costs, said now

- **A small restoration.** Each side of the loop, the ring's top band crosses the mask's lower edge (about 400 ×
  170 px a side). As the ring tilts about its hinge, that band drops toward the hinge line, by at most about 50 source px
  at 40°. The mask strip it uncovers is filled from the mask's own surrounding pixels, the way the elephant clock's
  plate was restored. The fill is only shown in that sliver while the ring is lifted, and never at rest. The ring
  itself is never redrawn.
- **Use unknown.** The Freer doesn't record what it was fixed to. Fittings of this size were fixed to lacquered
  wood: chests, coffins, small doors. The door pushou of palaces and tombs are the same mask and
  ring, larger. The README says so plainly ("In life / Here") and doesn't claim it hung on a palace door.
- **Tilt is kept moderate (≤ about 40°).** Beyond that, a round-section ring would show its rim, which wasn't
  photographed. Within it, the ring's own inlaid face, foreshortened, is a true view.
