Nuat thai · before your first session

Six questions, before the mat

What people ask us most, the week before they come. Open a question and the book unfolds to it.

What is Thai massage?

Nuat thai is given through clothes, on a mat on the floor, with no oil. The therapist presses with palms, thumbs, forearms, knees and feet along the sen, the lines drawn in the old manuals, and moves you through slow, assisted stretches.

It is an old craft. Its manuals were copied in the temples and households of Siam, and the school at Wat Pho still teaches from figures carved in its walls in the 1830s. A session is quiet and unhurried: you do very little except breathe, and say when something is too much.

What should I wear?

Loose, soft clothes you can move in: a T-shirt and drawstring trousers. We keep clean sets in every size. Leave rings and watches in the locker.

How long is a session?

Sixty, ninety or a hundred and twenty minutes. Ninety is the usual first session.

Will it hurt?

It should feel firm, at times strong, never sharp. Say "softer" or "stronger" and it changes at once.

You may feel a little tender the next day.

Can I change or cancel?

Yes, up to a day before, at no charge. Later than that we keep half the fee. If you are unwell, stay at home and call: we would rather see you next week.

Is it safe in pregnancy, or with an injury?

Tell us when you book, and again when you arrive. In pregnancy we work with you lying on your side, on cushions, and leave out deep work on the belly and the strong points of the legs; from the second trimester on we are glad to see you, with your midwife's blessing.

After recent surgery, or with a clot, high blood pressure or brittle bones, ask your doctor first. A sore knee or shoulder we work around, not on.

Anything else, ask at the desk, or write to us the day before. We read every note.
