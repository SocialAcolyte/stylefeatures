## The board: Met 01.4.1a, faience, Abydos, reign of Thutmose III

**Gameboard and Gaming Pieces**, ca. 1479–1425 BCE, from tomb D99 in cemetery D at Abydos (Egypt Exploration Fund),
Gift of the EEF, 1901, CC0. Thirty tiles of turquoise and pale blue faience, set by the museum into a modern wooden
box (43 × 13.5 cm). The bottom row is the end of the game, and it is marked: square 26 with three *nefer* signs, 27
with water, 28 with a fragment of a sign. Several tiles are mended from fragments, and five carry a round blue
patch. All of it is shown as it is.

- `01.4.1a_EGDP013652.jpg`: the senet face straight down, 4000 × 2250, the box top 3245 px across (75 px/cm), tiles
  250 px. Its edges are straight to within 0.2° of the frame, so it is cropped, not rotated.
- Its own pieces, from the same campaign (EGDP013654–13665), lying on the same grey, several of them **straight
  down**: cones as a spiral of black in blue around the tip, spools as flat marbled discs.
- DP310299 (a later campaign, three-quarter) shows those pieces standing on that board. That fixes the scale: a
  piece is about half a tile across (about 1.65 cm).

### Throw sticks

Met 544500 and 559437–559444 are lovely (ivory, tinted red on one face). The brief says to use them only if
they earn their place. They don't here: the pager moves where the reader asks, so a throw would be decoration. What
the sticks give is the rule of the motion: a throw is one to five, so a move of one to five squares is counted out
square by square, and anything longer is lifted and carried.
