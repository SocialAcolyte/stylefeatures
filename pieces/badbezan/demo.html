Twenty sticks on one pin

A fan from Tehran, dated 1301 of the Hijra, 1883 to 1884. It has no leaf: each of its twenty sticks is a slip of wood, painted, gilded and lacquered, and each carries its own panels of verse. A red silk ribbon runs through slots in every stick and holds them in their order on a single brass pin.

VIII

The rose has flushed red, the bud has burst,And drunk with joy is the nightingale—Hail, Sufis! lovers of wine, all hail!For wine is proclaimed to a world athirst.Like a rock your repentance seemed to you;Behold the marvel! of what availWas your rock, for a goblet has cleft it in two!

Bring wine for the king and the slave at the gate!Alike for all is the banquet spread,And drunk and sober are warmed and fed.When the feast is done and the night grows late,And the second door of the tavern gapes wide,The low and the mighty must bow the head’Neath the archway of Life, to meet what ... outside?
