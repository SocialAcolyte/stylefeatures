Street characters of Suzhou, 1516

One autumn in 1516 the Suzhou painter Zhou Chen sat at his window with nothing to do, remembered the beggars he had seen in the city's streets, and painted them, one to a leaf, twenty-four in all. Someone later mounted the album as a handscroll. Half of it is here: twelve of the figures, his own note on why he made them, and what three readers wrote after him.

1 of 17Title, written by Zhao Heqin in 1962

A handscroll is read on a table, from right to left, about an arm's length at a time: the left hand unrolls the next part while the right hand rolls up what has been seen, so the picture comes as a journey rather than all at once. Readers added to it as it passed through their hands. Huang Jishui wrote in 1564 that viewers fall about laughing at Zhou's beggars, and wished Zhou were alive to paint the men who beg for riches day and night. Zhang Fengyi thought that anyone who could look at them without pity had no kind heart. Wen Jia, in 1577, said that what each of them saw in it was their own business, and that the brushwork was beyond anyone else.
