    Turn the disc while you wait. Let go and the work takes it back.

    Peter Apian printed the Astronomicum Caesareum in Ingolstadt in 1540 for the emperor Charles V, and coloured, cut and assembled its paper instruments in his own workshop. On this one a paper disc, tied at its centre, turns over the zodiac. Its dragon's head and tail mark the two points where the moon's path crosses the sun's; set the head against the year, read the moon's latitude off the arcs.

    The nodes go backwards through the signs, one full turn in eighteen and a half years. Here the turn is the work: a value turns the disc that far, and at the end the head is home where it was printed.
