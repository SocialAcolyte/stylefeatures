# Pick note: sf-fan (the peacock piece), 2026-11-07

Connor on the first peacock (a Canton feather fan): "make the peacock one a bit better, closer to the original fan
style (fuller, wider). I don't want it MADE of peacock, I want it stylised to one." So the brief became a rigid
brisé, full and wide, opening to a half circle or more, with the peacock as its decoration or its very design. The
test stays the same: every state is the photograph's own pixels, rigid sticks turning about a real pivot, nothing
drawn.

## The hunt (second pass)

Sheets and files are in `tools/engine/jobs/`:
- `fan-peacock2-hunt/`: the Met v1.1 search ("peacock fan", "brisé peacock", "fan peacock brisé"), 159 objects. The
  only fans in it are leaf fans and brisé fans with birds on branches (209908). There is no peacock brisé in the public domain.
- Cooper Hewitt, retried slowly: every "peacock" fan query returns the same single fan, 1926-22-113.
- Commons in nine languages (éventail paon, waaier pauw, Fächer Pfau, ventaglio pavone, abanico pavo real, hiōgi,
  celluloid/horn/sandalwood/lacquer peacock fans, "fan in the shape of a peacock"). Results are in
  `fan-peacock2-hunt/sheet-a.jpg` and `sheet-b.jpg`.
- The first pass is in `fan-peacock-hunt/`, `fan-peacock-commons/` and `fan-peacock-birds/` (feather fans, live peacocks,
  paintings; see the shortlist below).

## Shortlist

1. **Kunstnerviften (the Artists' Fan), Kristiania (Oslo), 1898. Nasjonalmuseet for kunst, arkitektur og design,
   NG.M.04483. The museum's own photograph, 10661 × 5931, public domain (PD-old-100; CC-PD-Mark). Picked.**
   Engine inspect: pass.
2. Georges Bastard, *Éventail aux paons*, 1913, Musée d'Orsay (Commons, CC BY 3.0). This is exactly "stylised to one":
   mother-of-pearl and horn brisé sticks carved as a peacock's tail. But the only photograph is a 2020 px gallery
   snapshot through glass, at an angle, with a reflection across it. It fails "straight on, plain ground, sharp".
3. Brisé fan, China for Europe, 1780–1800, Cooper Hewitt 1926-22-113 (CC0, 3000 px). Ivory sticks pierced with
   peacocks in medallions. The peacocks are tiny among the pierced garlands, it opens to only about 110°, and the
   piercing would make every stick lace.
4. *Fan lace* (Commons, public domain, 4051 px). A lace fan leaf with three peacocks. It has no sticks, so nothing turns.
5. The first pass's feather fans (Museum Rotterdam 70303, Ashmolean 1930.451). Connor didn't want a fan made of
   peacock. Live peacocks fail the test (the train lies at many depths, and closing it uncovers unphotographed train).

## Why Kunstnerviften

- It is a wide, full brisé fan: eighteen broad wooden sticks with round tips, opening to a half circle and more
  (171° from guard to guard), like badbezan and larger.
- The peacock is its design. Across the gorge of every stick a peacock spreads its painted tail into a half circle
  of eyes over the whole fan, and its body sits at the pin. Above it, each stick is a painting by a different artist
  of the Kristiania circle: Hans Gude, Christian Krohg, Gerhard Munthe, Eilif Peterssen, Asta Nørregaard, Amaldus
  Nielsen and others. There are self-portraits, the fjord, sails, a dog and a sleigh.
- The photograph is sharp and straight on, on a plain grey linen mount, and very large (worked at half size, shipped at 2599 px).
- **Which photograph covers which state.** There is one photograph, the fan fully open, and it covers every state.
  Each stick lies on the one before it, clockwise, and the black lacquered top guard lies over all. Closing turns each
  stick back over the one below, so any opening shows only a subset of what the open photograph shows. The peacock's
  tail folds away into the stack the way it does in the hand. The Commons series by Sailko (`Artisti vari, ventaglio
  con stecche dipinte, 1898, 01–04`) is a second campaign, used as a reference only: oblique, CC BY-SA.
- The rivet is sharp: a brass pin with a brass bail hanging from it. The bail does not turn; the sticks' scalloped
  heels below the pin turn with their sticks.
- One culture, its real craft: Norwegian painters of the 1890s, each signing a stick, around a peacock in the
  Aesthetic manner. That is Whistler's peacock decade, so the Peacock Room demo stays.
