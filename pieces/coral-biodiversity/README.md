# coral-biodiversity

Haeckel's sea anemones arrive as line and bloom into full colour as you scroll, ending as the Actiniae plate itself.

## The real thing

The Actiniae plate from Kunstformen der Natur (1899) gathers sea anemones the way a reef gathers them — crowded, various, every tentacle drawn. Haeckel's lithographs were printed and then coloured toward his watercolour originals, so a plate like this was made in two passes: line, then colour. This scroll makes those two passes its whole action, washes flooding in wet-on-wet over the engraved forms until the reef stands complete and the final frame is the plate untouched.

## How it works

Layered copies of one scan are stacked in a pinned frame on a scroll runway — line first, then colour with a wet sheen that swells mid-scroll and settles — driven by a damped spring that follows the scroll and reverses cleanly. Reduced motion shows the finished plate. `coralBiodiversity(runway, opts)` returns a handle with `destroy()`.
