The cut of the pen

Before a calligrapher writes a word, the pen has to be made. It is a reed, seasoned for months, and it is cut fresh for every sitting: opened along its length with a small knife, pared on both sides, split, and then cut across the tip at the angle the script asks for. Everything that work needs was carried in one painted box.

The last cut is the one that decides the letters. It is made on a little plate of bone, the maqtaʿ, with one firm press of the knife, and the angle belongs to the script: one gives the even strokes of naskh, another the swell and hairline of nastaʿlīq. A practised hand cuts again whenever the edge softens, so the plate and the knife stay in the box beside the pens.

The ink sits at the far end of the tray, in a small well of silver or brass with a tuft of raw silk inside it. The silk holds the ink like a sponge, and the pen takes up only what it needs. A little spoon adds water when the ink thickens.

a couplet

Rumi, near the start of the Masnavi: the pen ran on and on in the writing, and when it came to love, it split in two. The line turns on the reed itself: every qalam is split down its nib, a slit made with the knife so that the ink runs to the tip.
