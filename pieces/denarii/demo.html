Counted out in silver

The denarius was Rome's silver coin for four and a half centuries, first struck around 211 BC and marked X for the ten bronze asses it was worth. Its half, the quinarius, was marked V. Each was struck by hand, one blank at a time, between two engraved dies, so no two came out alike.

Here every value is counted out in fives on a wool tunic woven in Roman Egypt, the way a money-changer laid coins on a cloth. Each coin stands for the same amount, which the key gives: two years, three or four, whatever lets the longest row fill the cloth. A real quinarius stands for half of it. Hover a coin to count up to it. Tap one to turn it over.

The waters of Rome

In AD 97 Sextus Julius Frontinus was made curator of Rome's water supply. He had the aqueducts surveyed, found where their water was being stolen, and wrote it all down.

His lengths are in paces, a thousand to the Roman mile.
