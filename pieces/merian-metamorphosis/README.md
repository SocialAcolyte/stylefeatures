# merian-metamorphosis

Merian's pomegranate plate arrives as bare engraving and takes its hand-colouring as you scroll, ending as the finished plate of 1705.

## The real thing

Maria Sibylla Merian sailed to Suriname in 1699 and came home with the observations behind Metamorphosis Insectorum Surinamensium (1705): each plate an insect's whole life — egg, caterpillar, chrysalis, moth — arranged on the plant it eats. The copperplates were printed uncoloured and painted by hand, copy by copy, which is exactly the journey this scroll repeats: the engraved line first, then washes flooding in wet-on-wet until the blue morpho and the pomegranate stand coloured, and the last frame is the plate itself.

## How it works

Layered copies of one scan are stacked in a pinned frame on a scroll runway — engraved line, then hand-colour washes with a wet sheen that swells mid-scroll and settles — driven by a damped spring that follows the scroll and reverses cleanly. Reduced motion shows the finished plate. `merianMetamorphosis(runway, opts)` returns a handle with `destroy()`.
